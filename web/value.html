Does cheapness predict returns?

Each month, every stock is ranked on each measure. The information coefficient (IC) is the rank correlation between that ranking and the next month's return: 0 means no information, and 0.05 is considered useful in practice. The t-stat says how unlikely that average is by chance; above about 2 is meaningful. The spread is how much the top fifth beat the bottom fifth per year, before trading costs.

How the scores are built, and the limits
Data
Annual income, cash-flow and revenue figures from 10-K filings, balance sheets and share counts from the latest 10-K or 10-Q, all from SEC EDGAR. A number is used only after its filing date. Prices from Yahoo Finance.
Earnings yield
Net income / market cap.
Book / price
Shareholders' equity / market cap.
Sales yield
Revenue / market cap.
FCF yield
(Operating cash flow − capital spending) / market cap.
EBIT / EV
Operating income / (market cap + long-term debt − cash). Banks and insurers often don't report it.
F-score
Nine yes/no tests from Piotroski (2000): profitable, cash-generative, improving return on assets, earnings backed by cash, falling leverage, rising liquidity, no dilution, rising gross margin, rising asset turnover.
Universe
The S&P 500 as listed in 2021. Companies acquired or delisted since then mostly have no price history and drop out, so results still carry some survivorship bias.
Not handled
Sector concentration (cheap stocks cluster in a few industries), annual-report lag of up to 15 months for income figures, and taxes.
